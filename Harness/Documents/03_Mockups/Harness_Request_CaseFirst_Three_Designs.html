DRAFT — Request page, case-first. Three ways to show the test cases a request produced. In all three the request itself shrinks to a header strip and the cases become the page. Every case shows its name, what it verifies in plain words, and its area and type. Same request throughout — 15 cases, 3 needing a decision — so the three compare directly. Prepared by the Business Analyst, 18 August 2026.

What a request produced

The earlier request mockups treated a request as the subject and the cases as a count. These invert it. Nothing here compares one request with another — the only question is what came out of this one, and what do I do about it?

D1

Grouped list — cases sorted by type

All 15 cases in one continuous list, grouped by test category. Cases needing a decision are pulled to a group at the top and marked with an amber rail. Reads top to bottom like a manifest of what was delivered.

What all three change The request stops being the subject. It shrinks to a header strip — key, one-line prompt, counts — and the cases occupy the page. Every case carries its name, a plain-language statement of what it checks, and its area and type, so a QA reader can judge the batch without opening anything.

A note on the shared labels. All 15 cases inherit the same feature and labels[] — only category differs between siblings. That is why the inherited set is stated once in the header and only the category appears per row. Repeating identical tags fifteen times would add noise without adding information.
Dependency, unchanged Grouping by category is the backbone of all three designs. As measured on the live system, category is default on all 313 cases. Until classification runs, D1 has one group, D2 has one column, and D3 has one value in its Type column. The plain-language "what it verifies" line is also new — nothing generates it today.
