What changed from DThree things, and they're the whole craft of it. The washes drop to roughly a third opacity. The gradient stops darken — #7C3AED→#22D3EE becomes #6D28D9→#0E7490 on the button, because bright aqua under white text only reaches about 2:1 contrast and fails. And depth switches from glow to soft shadow, since glow is invisible on white.
ColourLavender-white canvas, deep indigo ink, white cards with violet-tinted borders.
Where it wins over DReadable in a bright office and on a projector — dark themes wash out badly in demos. It also prints. And it keeps the modern gradient identity while staying closer to the light UI your team already uses.
Dev costSame as D if built together from one token set. Roughly double if the light theme is bolted on later, which is the usual mistake — decide now, not after.
J

Material Tonal — the one that ships fastest

Google's Material 3, seeded from a teal close to your existing brand. Depth comes from tonal layers — surfaces at slightly different tints of the same hue — rather than borders and shadows. The reason to consider it isn't looks: it's that every component here already exists, tested and accessible, in Material Web, MUI or Angular Material.

Two honest observations now that all ten exist. First, you are choosing two things, not one — a layout (C's lifecycle-first arrangement is the only one that fixes the workflow complaint) and a skin (any of the ten). Keeping those decisions separate will make the conversation with the dev team much shorter. Second, I is probably the better bet than D: it carries the same modern gradient identity, but dark themes wash out on projectors and in bright rooms, which is where you'll be presenting this.

My recommendation, unchanged in substance: C's structure with I's surface. Say the word and I'll build that as one screen, then write it up as a spec you can hand to the dev team.
